# News site editorial

Write for readers first, then make it easy for search and news surfaces to understand. Never invent facts, quotes, sources or dates; every claim in an article needs a source the reader can follow. Drafts only: do not publish or send anything.

## Articles that news surfaces can read

- **Headline**: specific, under about 70 characters, the main entity first, no clickbait or questions it does not answer. Write a separate social headline if it helps.
- **Dek**: one sentence that adds what the headline could not.
- **Freshness**: a visible published date and an updated date when facts change, with a one-line note of what changed.
- **Authorship and sources**: a named author or desk, and links to primary sources (agency mission pages, papers, official releases) in the body.
- **Images**: at least one large image (1200 px wide or more) with alt text and credit; Discover favours large images.
- **Structured data**: `NewsArticle` JSON-LD with headline, description, image, datePublished, dateModified, author, publisher, and mainEntityOfPage. Check it matches what the page shows; mismatches are worse than none.
- **Technical**: a canonical URL, a fast first render, no interstitials, and article pages listed in a news sitemap if the site has one.

When reviewing a page, report each item as present, missing or wrong, with the fix.

## Weekly newsletter digest

1. Pick 3 to 6 items from the week: the site's own articles first, then notable external news the readers would want, each with a source.
2. For each: a one-line headline, two or three sentences on why it matters to this audience (AI and machine learning in solar-system exploration), and the link.
3. Open with a two-line editor's note and close with one clear call to action (read, share or reply).
4. Subject line under about 50 characters plus a preview line; give two subject options.
5. Produce plain text and simple HTML. Keep the tone consistent with the site's voice notes if they exist.

Hand the draft to the board for approval before anything is scheduled or sent.
